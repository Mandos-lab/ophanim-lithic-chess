I · The Prophecy and the Discovery

Where the Code Came From

1866. A prophecy kept in the monastic archives of Mount Athos, published in 1975 by Bishop Grigorios of Messinia, said: “The twenty-four letters of the Alphabet, in due time, will be deciphered through great struggle.”

1975 to 1993. Theologos Anastasios Simaioforos read it, and spent about twenty years through the whole of ancient Greek literature: the Hesiodic cosmogony, the Homeric hymns, the classical dictionaries. He concluded that the alphabet is a complete and consistent cipher, and in 1993 he recorded and copyrighted the Alphabetical, Syllabic and Lexical Code under one title, ΕΛΛΗΝ ΛΟΓΟΣ.

Teaching. With his curator and colleague Barbara Stagiannou he taught it free of charge for thirteen years, five of them at the Laikon University of Athens (2004–2009). He presented the mathematics at the HERCMA conferences in 2003, 2005 and 2007 at the National Technical University of Athens, and ten textbooks followed through RAMAFA Editions.

Check. This section follows the handbook supplied with the slides. The research page, ellinlogos.net, sells the books (RA-MA-FA, the Lexicon Code, the Logic of Orthography, and decodings of the Labours of Heracles, Hesiod’s Theogony, Callimachus and Plato’s Timaeus) and does not itself name the conference dates.

II · Hellene

Έλλην, the Code says, is not a nationality. It reads ΕΛ, solar light, and ΛΗΝ, mind: a bright mind, φωτεινός νους. A Hellene is anyone, of any race, nation or culture, who feels kinship with light, seeks to wake the intellect, and stays loyal to truth. The usual question “where do you come from?” (από πού κατάγεσαι;) is split into από πού κάτω άγεσαι;, “from where above are you led down?”

Check. The pun is the slide’s own: κατάγεσαι (“you descend from”) is respelled κάτω άγεσαι (“you are led down”). The slide prints the first word as Έλλην, with a rough breathing mark on the epsilon.

III · The Architecture of Speech

Consonants, Vowels & the Spine

Plato noticed it in the Theaetetus and the Cratylus: the consonants (άφωνα, “voiceless”) have form but no voice, and the vowels (φωνήεντα, “voiced”) have voice but no body. In the Code the consonants are the silent scaffolding, the boundaries of nature. The vowels are the active sound, the voice and light of the mind that brings the scaffold into radiance.

All seven vowels are shown as manifestations of Alpha, the primordial solar radiation. They answer to the seven colours of light, the seven notes of the octave, and the seven cervical vertebrae that govern the voice. The seventeen thoracic and lumbar vertebrae answer to the seventeen consonants.

Αthe source, solar radiation
ΕAlpha expanding across space
ΙAlpha as a slender descending ray
Ηthe descent doubled into full revelation
Υaccumulated in great mass
Οbounded in a defined vessel
Ωseated upon the earth

Check. The anatomy holds: a person has 7 cervical, 12 thoracic and 5 lumbar vertebrae, so 12 + 5 = 17, and 7 + 17 = 24. The Code counts the classical 24 letters. In this house three older number-letters (stigma, koppa, sampi) are added, which makes 27, and the alphabet closes on sampi. See the “Counting the pebbles” note on The Mother’s Code and the spine on The Reed and the Code.

VI · Syllables

The Double Rhombus

A consonant with Alpha makes the first syllable. The Code sets these syllables in two rhombi, one inside the other: the upper for the heavenly energies and the lower for the material world. A rhombus stands for “anything that can move”, and the pair shows energy descending from the unseen into matter.

Upper rhombus, the heavenly syllables.

Check. Each list has nine syllables, and ΛΑ is in both: radiance of the stars above, liquids below, which is the double reading of Lambda. That gives 9 + 9 − 1 = 17 consonants, exactly the count of consonants in Greek. The lesson deck calls the upper list “the eight heavenly syllables”, but it lists nine.

An example, ΡΟΜΒΟΣ (rhombus). Ρ is the flow of supreme light, Μ is mother nature, Β is energy. Together the word says: anything that can move.

Why the syllable. Heraclitus saw nature make harmony from opposites, like positive and negative charge. Greek does the same by joining a consonant to a vowel to make the first syllable. As a cell is the basic unit of the body, the syllable is the basic unit of speech.

Check. The word ῥόμβος does mean a spinning top or magic wheel, and the verb ῥέμβομαι, to turn round, is related. The lesson deck gives the Code’s reading, which is a separate claim from that etymology.

How This Page Was Made

The letter meanings, the diphthongs and the decodings are from the handbook “Ellin Logos: The Geometric Alphabet Code, Part 1” and its lesson slides, which present the Simaioforos method. This page gives them as taught and does not test the method against historical linguistics. The closing octet (Rho to Omega) is an outline, not the author’s own text.
