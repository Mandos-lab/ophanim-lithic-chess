III · The Vertical Bisection

Φ, Zeus & the Me

The shift is encoded in Φ: the horizontal, cyclical circle bisected by a vertical line of masculine dominance. The sacred vitality of Zu was absorbed vertically into the sky-god Zeus.

After the felling, Inanna travels to Enki's Abzu in Eridu to secure the Me, the divine decrees and executable blueprints of civilisation: kingship, crafts, truth and war.

Linguists derive Zeus (Ζεύς, genitive Διός) from Proto-Indo-European *dyēus, “bright sky / day”, a cognate of Sanskrit Dyaus and Latin Jupiter (Dyeu-pater). A link to Sumerian zu is Karakin’s own proposal, not the standard etymology.

In Inanna and Enki she gets Enki drunk and leaves with over a hundred me, sailing them to Uruk in the Boat of Heaven. That poem is separate from the Huluppu story, so linking the two in sequence is the Codex's reading.

IV · The Physical Anchor

Mount Aragats & the Four Pillars

An isolated volcanic massif covering a fifth of Armenia: four peaks around a central crater. Its name is read as “The Atmosphere of Ara's Cauldron” (Autton Araatsoy).

In the 15th century BCE Thutmose III recorded a northern land where “heaven rests upon its four pillars”. Aragats is the Hara Berezaiti, the “High Watch” of the Avesta. Agni, “born of the rock”, is a memory of fire bursting from stone. Vishapakars, the dragon stones, mark the boundary of fire and water.

Confirmed: the massif covers about 6,000 km², roughly ⅕ of Armenia. It has four summits around a crater; the northern one, at 4,090 m, is Armenia's highest point.

Name: the traditional reading is “Ara's throne”, which scholars treat as folk etymology. I found no source for Autton Araatsoy, so cite yours.

Thutmose III: the older sources call the people Ermenen, not “Manin”. The “four pillars” line appears in older reference works such as the 1915 ISBE, and the Ermenen = Armenia identification is still debated.

Hara Berezaiti: the Avesta places it mythically, and identifying it with Aragats is Karakin’s thesis. Agni: Rigveda 2.1.1 does call him born “from the stone”.

Vishapakars: Chalcolithic, c. 4200–4000 BCE. They stand up to about 5.5 m tall and weigh several tonnes, some over four, not 7. They're set near springs and lakes, and Aragats has the biggest cluster, at Tirinkatar.

Light coagulates into matter

Μ · Mu · 40

N · Νοῦς: the open nasal release of immaterial consciousness. M · Ὁρατὴ Φύσις: the lips close and seal spirit into matter, and Mu = 40, the number of gestation and clay.

A capital M is two inverted V's, a double Λ. So the MM of Nammu is ΛΛΛΛ, four-fold radiance: when light is quadrupled, it slows into visible matter. The path runs from moon-god Nanna(r) to sea-goddess Nammu.

Confirmed: Μ = 40 in Greek numerals, and N is a nasal, airflow through the nose.

Nanna and Nammu are two distinct Sumerian deities: Nanna (Akkadian Sîn) is the male moon god of Ur, and Nammu is the primeval sea goddess and mother of Enki. No sound change links the two names, so the N→M path is a Codex reading, not a historical evolution. “Nannar” is an older transliteration of Nanna.

Real geology: this is supergene enrichment. Weathering oxidises sulfide ore, copper is carried away as blue sulfate, and gold stays behind, concentrated near the surface. Armenia's main gold deposits, such as Sotk and Amulsar, lie east and south of Aragats rather than on it.

Count the pebbles yourself

How to count, step by step

  1. Every Greek letter is also a number, its pebble. The first nine are the ones, the next nine the tens, the last nine the hundreds (table below).
  2. Type a word in the box in Greek letters, or tap the letters on the keyboard. The small number on each key is its pebble. Accents do not matter.
  3. Each letter becomes a tile with its number under it. The big number is all the tiles added together. By hand: write the number under each letter and add them up. Example, Κάδμος: 20 + 1 + 4 + 40 + 70 + 200 = 335.
  4. Read the small chips. 335 = 5 × 67 shows what the total is made of (a single number means it is prime). 37 × … appears when 37 divides the total. ≡ word means another word in the list adds up to exactly the same number: a pebble match.
