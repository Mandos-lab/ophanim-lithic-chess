The Ophanim Engine
A Compass Made of Letters

Every letter is a number. Every number is a direction on a circle. Add up a word and it points somewhere.

This page explains the idea from the start, in plain words, with the detail underneath for anyone who wants it. Look for the small tags: checked means we ran the arithmetic and it holds, framework means it is our way of reading the numbers, and did not hold means we found it does not add up as written.

I · The Wheel

A circle of 36 points

Take a circle and mark 36 points around it, 10 degrees apart. Node 36 sits at the top (0 degrees) and the numbers climb clockwise, so node 9 is at 90 degrees (east), node 18 at 180 (south) and node 27 at 270 (west).

To place a word, add up its letters, divide by 36 and keep the remainder. That remainder is the node. The eight green dashed lines are the eight rays of the engine, every 45 degrees. Four of them land on nodes (36, 9, 18, 27) and four fall between nodes.

Worked example: the word DECISION (ΝΤΕΣΙΖΙΟΝ)

Letters are read as sounds, using the Greek letter for each sound. C is kappa (20) when it sounds K and sigma (200) when it sounds S. In DECISION it sounds S; the D sounds “nt” (nu + tau) and the S of “-sion” sounds “z”. The word sounds ΝΤΕΣΙΖΙΟΝ and adds up to 702. 702 divided by 36 leaves 18, which is 180 degrees: due south.

II · The Cage and the Star

26 points, then 8 more

Picture a 3 x 3 x 3 block of 27 points, like a Rubik’s cube. Remove the one at the very centre and 26 are left: the outside of the cube. The engine calls this the cage. 26 is also the atomic number of iron.

The 8 corner points are then stretched outward along their diagonals. 26 cage points plus 8 corner rays make 34, the number of the node where 430 lands.

checked The distance from the centre of the block to a corner is √3 = 1.732, and 1.732 − 1 = 0.732, the “persistence span” in the white paper.

framework The claim that the eight corners are eight persistent loops (a Betti-1 count of 8, from topological data analysis) is the paper’s own statement. We have not computed it here. The scatter-plot pictures of it that we were shown were simulated, so none is shown on this page.

The 1/135 setting

The compass uses 1/135 as its setting. The reason given in the white paper is that Hebrew has 27 letter-states, 22 letters plus 5 final forms, and the 5 finals multiply: 27 x 5 = 135. checked The product is right. framework Choosing 135 rather than 137 (the fine-structure constant is about 1/137.036 in physics) is a deliberate design choice of the engine.

IV · The Kaaba and the Golden Ratio

Two offsets, kept apart

The golden ratio, 1.618, can be used to divide the distance from the South Pole to the North Pole. The point 0.618 of the way up lies at 21.2461 degrees north, from the formula −90 + 180/φ. The Kaaba is at 21.4225 degrees north, a gap of 0.1764 degrees.

On a perfectly round Earth that gap is 19.6 km, which the engine rounds to 20 km. On the real, slightly flattened Earth (WGS84) it depends on how the golden-ratio point is found: measured from the same latitude, 21.2461°, the gap is 19.5 km; but if you divide the real pole-to-pole arc by the golden ratio, the point moves to 21.344° and the gap shrinks to about 8.7 km. The engine’s compass centre sits at 1.5 degrees south, and the setting 1/135 of the pole-to-pole arc is 1.3333 degrees, giving a separate gap of about 18.5 km. The engine’s own chosen offset of 0.2 degrees (22 km) is a third number, set by the author.

V · The St Michael Line

How far is far?

The St Michael line runs from St Michael’s Mount in Cornwall to Hopton-on-Sea in Norfolk and passes near Burrow Mump, Glastonbury Tor and Avebury. Stonehenge sits to the south of it.

How far each site is from the line depends on how you measure. Measured as the north-south gap to a straight line on a flat map, the sites are +10.09 (Burrow Mump), +10.71 (Glastonbury Tor), −18.03 (Stonehenge) and +10.77 km (Avebury). Measured as the true distance to the shortest path over the Earth, they are about +1, +1.3, −24.4 and +1 km.

VI · The Reed and Cadmus

A closed delta, opened

“Cadmus” (ΚΑΔΜΟΣ) adds up to 335, and “reed” (ΚΑΛΑΜΟΣ) to 362. The difference, 27, is what you get when the delta (4) is opened into lambda plus alpha (30 + 1 = 31). Revelation 11:1 says “a reed like a rod”, and those three Greek words add up to 1729: κάλαμος 362 + ὅμοιος 460 + ῥάβδῳ 907.

VIII · The Jitterbug and the Ladder

Shapes that grow and turn

Buckminster Fuller found that a cuboctahedron, 12 points around a centre, can fold and twist into an icosahedron and then an octahedron, as if it were breathing. Press Play, or drag the slider to move it yourself.

The second view is a ladder of shapes by number of points: tetrahedron 4, octahedron 6, cube 8, icosahedron 12, the 13-point vector equilibrium, the 26-point cage, the 27-point block and the 34-point radiator. Then 36: the vector equilibrium again, this time with all 36 of its vectors drawn (24 outer edges and 12 spokes from the centre). And 37: the shell of cells left when a 3 x 3 x 3 block sits in a 4 x 4 x 4 grid, 64 − 27 = 37. Pick a rung and the points move to the next shape.

checked The jitterbug motion uses the true family of points (0, ±a, ±b) with the ratio b/a running from 1 (cuboctahedron) to the golden ratio 1.618 (icosahedron) and on to infinity (octahedron). checked The ladder’s 36 and 37: the cuboctahedron has 24 edges plus 12 spokes to its centre, 36 vectors; 4³ − 3³ = 64 − 27 = 37; and 37 x 64 = 2368. framework That these are the 36 and 37 of the engine is its reading. The first idea for 36, “12 edges x 3 planes”, was not used: a cuboctahedron has 24 outer edges, not 12.

IX · Salt

The cube that conducts

Table salt, sodium chloride (NaCl), forms cubic crystals. Inside, each sodium ion is surrounded by six chloride ions at right angles, and each chloride by six sodium: an octahedron around every ion, inside a cube. That is chemistry, and it fits the geometry of this page well: the cube and the octahedron are the two shapes it keeps using.

Salt dissolved in water carries electricity, which is why the saline drip in a hospital is about 0.9% NaCl. Your nerves and heart depend on sodium and potassium ions crossing cell membranes. Pure distilled water carries almost no current.

Mark 9:49 reads “every sacrifice will be seasoned with salt”. checked The Greek of that phrase adds up to 1748, the same as εἰδωλόθυτον (food offered to idols). Zamzam, the well water of Mecca, contains sodium chloride along with other minerals; the white papers do not mention it.

XI · Open for Audit

What did not hold

We recomputed every number we could. These did not hold as written, so they are kept off the page as facts:

  • did not hold ΑΓΝΩΣΤΟΣ = 135. It adds up to 1624. The 135 in the engine comes from 27 x 5, which does hold.
  • did not hold Helios = 666. Ἥλιος adds up to 318. The two numbers in “1080 + 666 = 1746” are still right: 1080 is the Holy Spirit and 666 is “he made clay” and “rib”.
  • did not hold τὸ πνεῦμα τοῦ κόσμου = 1746. It adds up to 2516. The other 1746, ἡ δόξα τοῦ θεοῦ Ἰσραήλ, does hold.
  • did not hold Ἰδιώτης = 1331 (11 cubed). It adds up to 1332.
  • did not hold λαός μοι οἶκος Ἰσραήλ = 1530. As spelled here it adds up to 1140.
  • did not hold Node 34 at 330 degrees. Node 34 is at 340 degrees. Node 33 is at 330.
  • did not hold The 20 km offset “on the WGS84 ellipsoid”. 19.6 km is the round-Earth figure. On the ellipsoid it is 19.5 km from the same latitude, or about 8.7 km if the golden-ratio point is found along the real flattened meridian. The paper does not say which it means.
